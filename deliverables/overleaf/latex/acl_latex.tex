\begin{abstract}
We reproduce and extend KELE \citep{peng2025kele}, a multi-agent Socratic teaching framework, and contribute on three axes: (i) a \emph{fusion} architecture collapsing consultant and teacher onto a single open-weight backbone through a structured-output call, eliminating API spend; (ii) a 24M-parameter BERT classifier replacing the LLM consultant, paired with a Gemma~4 31B teacher and 10-shot exemplars --- our locked $n{=}681$ headline at $\mathbf{48.15\%}$ state accuracy / $36.78$ R-1, a $1.86\times$ lift over the GPT-4o + SocratTeachLLM baseline; and (iii) \textbf{a methodological critique showing the published benchmark systematically rewards training-data memorization over teaching capability}. When configurations are ranked by surface-form metrics ($\text{R-1}+\text{R-2}+\text{BLEU-4}$), the 9B SocratTeachLLM fine-tune outscores Opus~4.6 with carefully tuned prompt engineering by $+10.83$ on the sum; when the same configurations are ranked by pedagogical state accuracy, SocratTeachLLM falls to last place (25.94\%, below even raw Opus at 39.75\%) and our open-weight Gemma + top-3 stack reaches first (50.72\%). The two rankings invert. The gap widens monotonically with $n$-gram length ($+1.59$ R-1, $+4.92$ R-2, $+4.07$ BLEU-4), the strongest possible memorization signature. A cross-lingual translation experiment confirms the diagnosis: running SocratTeachLLM against an English translation of the dataset reproduces the original paper's flagship $\text{R-1} = 57.40$ headline within $1.5$ points (we measure $55.85$), while LLM-judge transfer shows frontier+prompt-engineering configurations lose only $-0.07$ across the language shift versus SocratTeachLLM losing $-1.0$ ($14\times$ degradation on a paraphrase-invariant metric). We propose a four-metric replacement panel --- LLM-judge rubric, state accuracy against an independently-trained BERT annotator, semantic embedding similarity, and stage-progression efficiency --- implement it, and find our open-weight integration tops the memorization-resistant ranking ($8.19/10$ judge composite). A complementary bootstrap convergence analysis across all seven of our full-scale $n{=}681$ runs shows that the test split is over-sampled: $n{=}400$ random dialogues suffice to estimate all four metrics within $\leq 2$ percentage points of the full-$n$ truth across every configuration tested ($41\%$ compute saving with no loss of ranking precision). We also release \textbf{SocratDataset-EN}, a complete English translation of the dataset (\S\ref{sec:dataset-en}). Code and data are public.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Dialogue plays a central role in shaping learning outcomes. Traditional knowledge-imparting instruction places students in a passive role, delivering answers directly without engaging deeper reasoning \citep{johnston1994teaching}. Socratic teaching offers an alternative: through structured questioning, learners are guided to actively construct knowledge and discover answers themselves \citep{seeskin1987socratic, chang1998socratic}. Research consistently shows that Socratic dialogue promotes cognitive development and deeper understanding \citep{knezic2010socratic}, yet its reliance on highly skilled instructors makes it difficult to scale in practice.

Recent advances in large language models (LLMs) have opened a path toward automating Socratic instruction. LLMs are increasingly applied in educational settings---student Q\&A, lesson planning, and adaptive tutoring \citep{kasneci2023chatgpt, gan2023llmedu}---and several systems have explored Socratic-style dialogue generation, including prompt-based approaches \citep{zhang2024spl, alhossami2024socratic} and fine-tuned models such as SocraticLM \citep{liu2024socraticLM} and EduChat \citep{dan2023educhat}. However, these efforts generally focus on surface-level heuristic questioning while neglecting the structured, multi-stage progression that characterizes effective Socratic pedagogy.

\citet{peng2025kele} address this gap with KELE (Knowledge-Enlightened Learning Enhanced by LLMs), a multi-agent framework that decomposes Socratic teaching into five hierarchical stages---student questioning, concept probing, inductive reasoning, rule construction, and teacher summary---governed by a formal rule system called SocRule containing 34 teaching strategies. The framework employs a ``consultant--teacher'' mechanism: a \emph{consultant} agent evaluates student cognitive state and selects the appropriate teaching strategy, while a \emph{teacher} agent generates the actual dialogue response. To power the teacher agent, the authors fine-tune GLM4-9B \citep{glm2024chatglm} using LoRA \citep{hu2022lora} on SocratDataset, a corpus of 6,803 dialogues with over 42,000 annotated turns, producing SocratTeachLLM---a model that outperforms GPT-4o across all nine Socratic teaching evaluation dimensions despite being significantly smaller.

In this work, we reproduce and extend the KELE framework with four goals:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Reproduction.} We reimplement the consultant--teacher pipeline, serve SocratTeachLLM locally via vLLM \citep{kwon2023vllm}, and use GPT-4o \citep{openai2024gpt4o} as the consultant to evaluate on the same 681-dialogue test split. We report ROUGE \citep{lin2004rouge}, BLEU \citep{papineni2002bleu}, and per-stage state accuracy to verify the original claims.
    \item \textbf{Diagnosis.} We analyze where the reproduction matches or diverges from the published results, with particular attention to the low mid-stage state accuracy (stages c and d below 6\%) that emerges from our evaluation.
    \item \textbf{Improvement.} We propose replacing the LLM-based consultant with a lightweight supervised classifier trained on SocratDataset's 42,000 state-labeled turns, targeting the system's weakest link: the consultant's ability to correctly identify student cognitive states during the complex middle stages of Socratic dialogue. Our locked open-weight system (BERT consultant + Gemma 4 31B teacher + 10-shot stage-balanced exemplars) reaches $\mathbf{48.15\%}$ state accuracy at $n{=}681$, a $\mathbf{1.86\times}$ lift over the GPT-4o + SocratTeachLLM baseline.
    \item \textbf{Benchmark critique.} We show that the surface-form metrics under which KELE is evaluated systematically reward training-data memorization over teaching capability. The two rankings (surface-form vs.\ state accuracy) invert across the same configurations: SocratTeachLLM tops the published metrics but produces the worst state accuracy ($25.94\%$, below even raw Opus 4.6 at $39.75\%$), while frontier-model-with-prompt-engineering configurations win state accuracy but lose surface-form. The gap widens with $n$-gram length, the signature of phrasing-level memorization. A cross-lingual translation experiment (running the dataset through English) reproduces the original paper's $\text{R-1}=57.40$ headline within $1.5$ points and produces a $14\times$ asymmetric degradation under LLM-judge transfer for SocratTeachLLM-using configurations vs.\ frontier+prompt-engineering configurations, confirming language-bound memorization. We propose a four-metric replacement panel (LLM-judge rubric, state accuracy against an independently-trained BERT annotator, semantic embedding similarity, and stage-progression efficiency), implement it, and find our open-weight integration tops the memorization-resistant ranking. A complementary bootstrap convergence analysis across all seven of our full-scale $n{=}681$ runs further shows that the test split is over-sampled for ranking purposes: $\boldsymbol{n{=}400}$ random dialogues suffice to estimate all four metrics within $\boldsymbol{\leq 2}$ percentage points of the full-$n$ truth across every configuration tested, cutting per-run compute, wall clock, and API spend by roughly $41\%$ with no loss of decision precision at any ranking boundary $\geq 2$ pp. We recommend the four-metric panel evaluated at $n{=}400$ as the new defensible standard for Socratic-teaching research.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Large Language Models in Education}

LLMs are rapidly being integrated across educational applications, from student Q\&A and personalized tutoring to lesson planning and intelligent feedback \citep{kasneci2023chatgpt, gan2023llmedu}. InstructGPT-style alignment through reinforcement learning from human feedback has enabled conversational agents that can adapt explanations to student needs \citep{chung2024scaling}. RetLLM-E \citep{mitra2024retllm} retrieves relevant course material to ground LLM responses for student forum questions, while LessonPlanner \citep{fan2024lessonplanner} assists novice teachers in generating pedagogically sound lesson plans. The CLASS framework \citep{sonkar2023class} builds intelligent tutoring systems grounded in learning science principles, guiding students step-by-step through interactive dialogue. These systems demonstrate the growing capacity of LLMs to serve as educational tools, though most operate in a single-agent paradigm without explicit pedagogical structure.

\subsection{Technology-Enhanced Socratic Teaching}

The Socratic method---teaching through guided questioning rather than direct instruction---has a long history in educational research \citep{seeskin1987socratic, chang1998socratic}. Technology-enhanced implementations range from early rule-based dialogue systems \citep{graesser2004autotutor} to modern LLM-powered approaches. \citet{alhossami2024socratic} demonstrate that LLMs can employ Socratic questioning to help students debug code, while \citet{shridhar2022mathword} show automatic generation of Socratic sub-questions for math word problems. The SPL system \citep{zhang2024spl} leverages GPT-4 for Socratic dialogue in a playground-style interface.

On the fine-tuning side, EduChat \citep{dan2023educhat} and SocraticLM \citep{liu2024socraticLM} train models on Socratic-style datasets to internalize heuristic questioning patterns, while \citet{ding2024socraticmath} apply the Socratic method specifically to conversational mathematics teaching. MathDial \citep{macina2023mathdial} contributes a tutoring dialogue dataset grounded in math reasoning with rich pedagogical annotations.

However, as \citet{peng2025kele} observe, these approaches primarily focus on generating heuristic questions at the individual turn level without enforcing coherent multi-stage teaching progression. The absence of structured rules can lead to arbitrary guidance, abrupt topic shifts, and failure to consolidate learning. KELE addresses this through its five-stage SocRule system and dual-agent architecture, making it---to our knowledge---the first framework to systematically implement structured Socratic teaching with LLMs.

\subsection{Multi-Agent Collaborative Systems}

Multi-agent architectures have emerged as an effective paradigm for complex LLM tasks that benefit from separation of concerns \citep{ge2023openagi}. In the educational domain, the key insight of KELE's consultant--teacher design is that teaching \emph{planning} (what stage and strategy to use) and teaching \emph{execution} (generating the actual dialogue) are fundamentally different capabilities that benefit from distinct agents \citep{peng2025kele}. This mirrors real-world educational practice where teaching consultants advise on instructional strategy while classroom teachers handle direct student interaction \citep{nevin2009collaborative}. Single-agent approaches struggle with both responsibilities simultaneously, often producing responses that are individually reasonable but lack coherent pedagogical progression \citep{an2024llmcontext}. Our work investigates whether the consultant role---essentially a state classification task---can be more effectively handled by a specialized classifier rather than a general-purpose LLM.

\section{Dataset and Methodology}

\subsection{SocratDataset}

SocratDataset \citep{peng2025kele} comprises 6,803 multi-turn Chinese dialogues totalling 42,892 annotated interaction turns, drawn from elementary school science education. Each dialogue simulates a Socratic tutoring session in which a teacher guides a student from an initial question to a correct, self-discovered answer. Every turn is annotated with one of 35 pedagogical states organized across five progressive teaching stages (Table~\ref{tab:socrule}). Dialogues average 6.3 turns (range 5--12). The dataset is split 90/10 into 6,122 training dialogues and 681 test dialogues (seed = 42), following the original paper's partition.

\subsection{SocratDataset-EN: English Translation}
\label{sec:dataset-en}

As a secondary contribution, we produced \textbf{SocratDataset-EN}, a complete English translation of all 6,803 SocratDataset dialogues (42,892 turns). SocratDataset is natively Chinese; no English version existed, which limits access to the corpus for researchers outside the Chinese NLP community and constrains the fine-tuning tooling available for a teacher base-model upgrade.

The translation was generated by a local Qwen3.5-9B instance served via llama.cpp, with $\text{temperature}=0.1$ and thinking disabled for near-deterministic output. A two-tier checkpointing system (local every 5 records, HuggingFace every 50 records) enabled the pipeline to resume overnight runs without data loss. Per-record validation enforced JSON structure, turn-count matching, required-field presence, and zero Chinese characters in the translated fields. Nine records that exhausted all automated retries were manually translated and are flagged in the dataset metadata via a distinct \texttt{translation\_meta.model} tag. Throughput was approximately 370--390 records per hour on an AMD Radeon R9700 (Q4 quantization). The dataset is publicly available at \url{https://huggingface.co/datasets/ulises-c/SocratDataset-EN}.

\paragraph{Bilingual deployment without bilingual training.} Chinese and English are typologically dissimilar---Chinese is analytic, topic-prominent, and logographic, while English is inflected, subject-prominent, and alphabetic---so a strict surface-form classifier would not transfer. However, the consultant we train and ship (see \S\ref{sec:proposed-improvements}) operates on a multilingual base model (Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B) whose deeper layers learn approximately language-agnostic semantic representations: under the next-token-prediction training objective, the cheapest internal encoding of cross-lingual training data is one in which semantically equivalent content in different languages maps to nearby points, leaving the language axis approximately separable from the meaning axis~\citep{pires2019multilingual,wu2019beto,conneau2020unsupervised}. Empirically in our own results (\S\ref{sec:claude-overfit}), Anthropic's Claude Sonnet/Opus configurations transferred from Chinese to English with a negligible $-0.07$ LLM-judge delta, while configurations relying on SocratTeachLLM (a 9B model fine-tuned monolingually) lost $\sim$14$\times$ more. The pedagogical state labels themselves (a0--e34) describe Socratic moves that are language-invariant: a student probing a question is the same pedagogical state in either language. The classifier thus targets the pedagogical labeling, not the surface lexicon. Because LoRA fine-tuning (small adapter rank, $\sim$0.3\% of base parameters) tends to preserve the base model's cross-lingual structure rather than overwrite it, we expect the Chinese-trained classifier to transfer to SocratDataset-EN with a modest performance drop rather than catastrophic failure. Bilingual co-training on the union of SocratDataset and SocratDataset-EN remains a possible future direction; we structure the present contribution so that empirical cross-lingual transfer of the Chinese-trained checkpoint can be measured first, and bilingual co-training pursued only if free transfer proves insufficient.

\subsection{SocRule: Five-Stage Teaching Framework}

KELE operationalizes Socratic pedagogy through SocRule, a rule system derived from the teaching science literature \citep{trowbridge2011teaching} that decomposes each dialogue into five strictly ordered stages:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item[\textbf{(a)}] \textbf{Student Questioning} (2 states). The student poses an initial question; the consultant determines whether the question is clear enough to proceed.
    \item[\textbf{(b)}] \textbf{Concept Probing} ($\leq$3 turns, 6 states). The teacher probes the student's prior knowledge and identifies misconceptions through diagnostic questions.
    \item[\textbf{(c)}] \textbf{Inductive Reasoning} ($\leq$5 turns, 22 states). The most complex stage: the teacher guides the student to form hypotheses, test predictions against evidence, and reason inductively toward correct explanations.
    \item[\textbf{(d)}] \textbf{Rule Construction} ($\leq$3 turns, 4 states). The student synthesizes observations into a general rule or definition, with the teacher providing corrective scaffolding as needed.
    \item[\textbf{(e)}] \textbf{Teacher Summary} (1 state). Once the student arrives at the correct answer, the teacher summarizes the key concepts and concludes the dialogue.
\end{enumerate}

Stage progression is strictly monotonic---once the dialogue advances from stage $s_i$ to $s_{i+1}$, it cannot regress. Each state maps to a concrete teaching strategy (e.g., ``provide a counterexample,'' ``ask the student to re-examine prior learning'') that dictates the teacher's pedagogical move. A maximum of 8 teaching rounds per dialogue is enforced; if the limit is reached before stage (e), the system forces a transition to the final rule-construction state (d33) to ensure graceful conclusion.

\subsection{System Architecture}
\label{sec:architecture}

KELE employs a consultant--teacher dual-agent architecture in which the two roles operate in sequence on every turn. Given a student utterance $u_t$ and the full dialogue history $H_{<t}$:

\paragraph{Consultant.} The consultant receives $(H_{<t}, u_t)$ along with the complete SocRule specification encoded in its system prompt. It outputs a structured JSON response containing (1)~an \emph{evaluation}: a free-text analysis of the student's current cognitive state, and (2)~a \emph{state code} (e.g., \texttt{c12}) identifying the applicable teaching state. The state code indexes into a deterministic state-to-action mapping that selects the corresponding teaching strategy.

\paragraph{Teacher.} The teacher receives $(H_{<t}, u_t)$ together with the consultant's evaluation and the action string for the selected state. It generates a natural-language response---typically a single Socratic question---that implements the prescribed strategy while maintaining an encouraging, age-appropriate tone.

\paragraph{Enforcement.} Our implementation enforces SocRule constraints programmatically: if the consultant proposes a state from an earlier stage than the current one, the system overrides the regression and retains the current stage. This phase-monotonicity guard ensures strict forward progression regardless of consultant errors.

\subsection{Reproduction Setup}

We reimplement the full KELE pipeline and evaluate on the 681-dialogue test split under the following configuration:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Teacher:} SocratTeachLLM (9.4B parameters; GLM4-9B \citep{glm2024chatglm} fine-tuned via LoRA \citep{hu2022lora} on SocratDataset's training split), served locally via vLLM \citep{kwon2023vllm} on an NVIDIA RTX 5090 (32\,GB).
    \item \textbf{Consultant:} GPT-4o-2024-11-20 \citep{openai2024gpt4o} via the OpenAI API, matching the original paper's consultant choice.
    \item \textbf{Inference:} Each test dialogue is run end-to-end through the live system. The consultant produces predictions from scratch on every turn---it does \emph{not} receive ground-truth state labels.
\end{itemize}

\noindent This last point constitutes the most significant methodological difference from the original evaluation. \citet{peng2025kele} feed ground-truth consultant labels directly to the teacher, measuring teacher generation quality in isolation. Our evaluation measures the full pipeline including consultant errors, producing a more realistic assessment of end-to-end system performance. As we show in Table~\ref{tab:reproduction}, this accounts for the majority of the ROUGE gap between our results and the published figures.

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}

We evaluate along two complementary axes:

\paragraph{Text overlap.} We compute ROUGE-1, ROUGE-2, and ROUGE-L \citep{lin2004rouge} using character-level tokenization (each Chinese character treated as a token), which is the standard convention for Chinese text evaluation. BLEU-4 \citep{papineni2002bleu} is computed with word-level segmentation. These metrics measure how closely the generated teacher responses match the ground-truth responses in the dataset.

\paragraph{State accuracy.} We compare the consultant's predicted state code against the ground-truth annotation for each turn, reporting both overall accuracy and per-stage breakdowns. State accuracy is a critical diagnostic metric: an incorrect state prediction causes the teacher to receive the wrong teaching strategy, which can produce pedagogically incoherent responses even when the teacher model itself is capable. As we report in Section~4, accuracy varies dramatically across stages---from 95\% on the binary stage~(a) to below 6\% on the 22-way classification in stage~(c)---motivating our proposed improvement of replacing the LLM consultant with a supervised classifier.

The central finding is that ROUGE and BLEU are \emph{insensitive} to consultant quality: a free local 7B model costs only $-$1.0 ROUGE-1 and $-$1.0 BLEU-4 relative to GPT-4o. State accuracy, by contrast, degrades sharply with consultant capability, particularly on stage~c (22 fine-grained inductive-reasoning states), which drops from 4.70\% (GPT-4o) to 0.63\% (Qwen2.5:7B). This confirms that the consultant bottleneck is a structured classification problem poorly suited to zero-shot prompting of general-purpose LLMs.

\subsection{Proposed Improvements}
\label{sec:proposed-improvements}

We target three complementary weaknesses identified in the baseline evaluation.

\paragraph{Hierarchical state classifier.}
We propose replacing the LLM consultant with a supervised two-stage classifier built on a pretrained Chinese BERT encoder \citep{devlin2019bert}. A first classification head predicts the teaching stage (5-way softmax); a second head predicts the within-stage state conditioned on the predicted stage (up to 22-way for stage~c). Both heads are trained jointly on the 42,000 labeled turns of the SocratDataset training split. A 125M-parameter classifier trained directly on in-domain labels should substantially outperform zero-shot LLM prompting on this structured prediction task while eliminating per-run API cost entirely.

\paragraph{Teacher base model upgrade.}
SocratTeachLLM is fine-tuned from GLM4-9B (C-Eval $\approx$81.5), which lags behind more recent Chinese-capable open-weight models. We propose re-running the SocratDataset LoRA fine-tune starting from \texttt{deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-14B} (C-Eval 91.8, MIT license), which inherits chain-of-thought reasoning behavior well-suited to multi-step Socratic dialogue. We will ablate against \texttt{Qwen/Qwen3-14B} (dense architecture, Apache 2.0) and the direct architectural successor of the current base, \texttt{zai-org/GLM-4-9B-0414}, to isolate the contribution of base-model capability from fine-tuning data quality.

\paragraph{Retrieval-augmented teacher.}
To address the surface-form gap that drives the BLEU-4 shortfall, we propose indexing the SocratDataset training split by topic and concept using a Chinese sentence encoder (\texttt{BAAI/bge-large-zh-v1.5}) and prepending the top-3 most similar past dialogues as few-shot exemplars at inference time. Retrieval injects corpus-specific phrasing style without additional fine-tuning and is composable with both the classifier and the model-upgrade improvements.

\section{Evaluation and Results}

This section reports the full experimental campaign behind our extension. We motivate the design choices forced by our compute budget (\S\ref{sec:budget}), describe the smoke--mini--full gating protocol (\S\ref{sec:protocol}), report the smoke matrix that selected our promotion candidate (\S\ref{sec:smoke}), the mini gate that validated it (\S\ref{sec:mini}), the headline full run (\S\ref{sec:full}), and the Gemma 4 31B pivot that was \emph{retracted at full scale} (\S\ref{sec:gemma}, $-7.31$ pts vs.\ A3B due to a $21\%$ schema-fallback rate). \S\ref{sec:takeaways} summarizes findings and \S\ref{sec:nextsteps} the planned next runs.

\subsection{Compute budget and architectural pivot}
\label{sec:budget}

All open-weight runs in this section execute on a single NVIDIA RTX 5090 (32\,GB VRAM) shared between consultant and teacher. The canonical KELE stack co-locates SocratTeachLLM ($\approx$19\,GB) with a frontier consultant; running this with even a 14B consultant exceeds the VRAM budget once KV cache and structured-output decode buffers are accounted for. This constraint precludes one of our originally proposed improvement axes (re-running KELE with a stronger Chinese-capable teacher), and it forces a single-backbone design choice: one open-weight model must serve both roles.

We exploit this constraint as a research opportunity. We introduce a \textbf{fusion} (unified-call) variant in which the consultant's structured state prediction and the teacher's natural-language response are produced by a \emph{single} JSON-schema-constrained LLM call. The schema requires fields $\{$\texttt{evaluation}, \texttt{state\_code}, \texttt{teacher\_response}$\}$ and is enforced server-side via \texttt{llama.cpp}'s strict \texttt{json\_schema} grammar. Fusion has two practical benefits in our setting: (i)~it halves the per-turn round-trip and KV-cache duplication relative to two-call inference, and (ii)~it enables the consultant to condition its state choice on the same chain-of-thought used to generate the teacher response, which we hypothesized would improve mid-stage classification.

\subsection{Smoke--mini--full gating protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

Each candidate configuration is gated through three escalating evaluation tiers before any full-test-set commitment:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Smoke} ($n{=}5$, $\sim$33 turns, $\sim$10--40 minutes): cheapest sanity check. Used to rank a wide configuration matrix.
    \item \textbf{Mini} ($n{=}25$, $\sim$145 turns, $\sim$30--60 minutes): stratified sample with state-distribution coverage. Used as the promotion gate to a full run.
    \item \textbf{Full} ($n{=}681$, $\sim$4{,}170 turns, multi-hour overnight): canonical evaluation on the 681-dialogue held-out test split.
\end{itemize}

\noindent A central methodological observation from our campaign is that smoke and mini results bracket the eventual full-run number from opposite directions: the smoke is consistently optimistic (small-$n$ favorable variance) and the mini is consistently pessimistic (stratified-sample worst-case). For our headline run (\S\ref{sec:full}), the smoke--mini \emph{average} ($+12.86$ vs.\ the realized $+12.76$) was the most accurate small-$n$ predictor of full-run state accuracy. We adopt this average as our promotion criterion going forward.

\paragraph{Findings from the smoke matrix.}
(1)~\emph{Fusion uniformly improves state accuracy} over two-call within each model and thinking mode (mean $\Delta{=}+9.9$ points). The single-call architecture's ability to condition state choice on the same reasoning trace used for response generation appears to matter more than the loss of an explicit consultant-then-teacher decomposition. (2)~\emph{Thinking mode helps within fusion}; it hurts within two-call. We attribute this to the fact that two-call's consultant has its own dedicated thinking budget, so a second thinking pass on the teacher provides no marginal information; in fusion the single thinking trace is shared across both decisions. (3)~\emph{A3B's MoE architecture pays off at scale}: 35B-A3B is $3\times$ faster per turn than 27B Q5 in fusion-think (7.0 vs.\ 21.0 minutes for $n{=}5$) because only $\sim$3B of its 35B parameters activate per token. We promote \textbf{A3B fusion-think} to the mini gate as the best speed--accuracy trade-off. The 27B fusion-think configuration is held in reserve as a high-accuracy fallback if A3B underperforms at full scale.

\paragraph{Smoke--mini average projection (\emph{retracted at full scale, see below}).} Applying the smoke--mini-average heuristic from \S\ref{sec:protocol}, Gemma's projected full-run state accuracy is $(51.52 + 41.89)/2 = 46.71\%$, a projected lift of $\boldsymbol{+20.77}$ points over the GPT-4o baseline --- $+8.01$ points beyond A3B's realized full-run lift. The original projection paragraph is preserved here to document the pre-result reasoning and frame the methodological finding.

\paragraph{Wall-clock projection.} At $22.1$\,s/turn at mini scale and applying the $\times 1.18$ mini-to-full margin observed for A3B, a Gemma full run is projected at $4{,}171 \times 22.1 \times 1.18 \approx 108{,}800$\,s $\approx$ \textbf{30.2 hours}, roughly $1.8\times$ A3B's full-run wall-clock. This fits in a single weekend slot.

\paragraph{Why Gemma appeared to beat A3B at small scale.} A3B activates only $\sim$3B of 35B parameters per token; this is what made it $\sim 1.85\times$ faster but also was hypothesized to cap its in-context reasoning depth on the 22-state stage~c classification. Gemma 4 31B is a dense 31B model, paying full per-token compute and at smoke and mini produced a sharper structured-state distribution, particularly visible on stage~c ($+6.4$ points at mini) and stage~d ($+7.0$ points at mini). The schema-fallback rate is $0/148$ at mini for Gemma, identical to A3B's $0/145$. \emph{This finding does not hold at scale}, as we report next.

\paragraph{Full-run result (2026-05-17): the projection failed.} The Gemma 4 31B fusion-think full run completed in $21.8$ hours wall clock (\textbf{faster than projected}), but landed at \textbf{$31.39\%$ overall state accuracy and $27.27$ ROUGE-1} --- a \textbf{$+5.45$-point lift} over the GPT-4o baseline, \textbf{$-7.31$ points below the locked A3B headline}, and a \textbf{$15.32$-point overshoot} of the smoke--mini average projection ($46.71\%$). Per-stage, Gemma loses to A3B on every stage (a: $-13.07$, b: $-6.18$, c: $-3.68$, d: $-0.55$, e: $-18.76$), with stages a and e showing the largest regressions. The smoke--mini average heuristic that landed A3B's full result within $0.10$ points failed catastrophically here. \textbf{The root cause is in the schema-fallback rate: Gemma fell back to two-call mode on $890/4{,}246 = 21.0\%$ of turns at full scale, vs.\ A3B's $38/4{,}171 = 0.91\%$.} The small-sample mini run ($n{=}148$ turns) was lucky --- it didn't hit the long-tail dialogues on which Gemma's strict-JSON adherence on stage-c-class structured output breaks down. This is the headline methodological finding of the campaign: \textbf{schema-fallback rate is a critical missing variable in cross-architecture scaling-prediction protocols}, and small-$n$ fallback rates can mask a $20\times$ degradation that only emerges at full scale. The framing of Gemma 4 31B as a ``headline forward candidate'' that supersedes A3B (proposed in this section on the basis of smoke/mini evidence) is hereby \textbf{retracted at full scale}. A3B fusion-think (\S\ref{sec:full}) remains the locked open-weight backbone, and the BERT-consultant integration (\S\ref{sec:bert-integration}) --- which removes the schema-fallback dependency by replacing the consultant LLM entirely --- becomes the active forward candidate for full-scale confirmation (\S\ref{sec:nextsteps}).

\subsection{Hierarchical stage classifier ablation}
\label{sec:bertclassifier}

Per the proposed Improvement~\#2 (\S\ref{sec:proposed-improvements}), we trained a Chinese BERT encoder as a drop-in 5-way stage classifier and evaluated it on the same 681-dialogue test split used for every LLM consultant configuration in this paper.

\paragraph{Setup.} Base model: \texttt{BAAI/bge-small-zh-v1.5} ($\approx$24M parameters), a much smaller encoder than the originally proposed 125M-parameter BERT. \paragraph{Result.} The full Gemma-teacher combo (BERT consultant + Gemma~4 31B teacher + 10-shot exemplars) lands at \textbf{$51.06\%$ state accuracy and $38.53$ ROUGE-1 at matched $n{=}50$} --- the highest open-weight configuration we measured on both metrics, with a $+25.12$-pt state-accuracy lift over the GPT-4o baseline and $\approx 86\%$ of GPT-4o's ROUGE-1 recovered ($38.53 / 44.61$). The A3B-teacher rows isolate component contributions: BERT alone (placeholder eval) matches the LLM consultant on state acc ($44.06\%$ vs.\ $44.15\%$), confirming the consultant component can be replaced by a $1{,}400{\times}$-smaller classifier without state-accuracy loss; adding 10-shot teacher exemplars on top of BERT recovers the surface-form penalty ($+7.21$ R-1 from placeholder to combo); and swapping the A3B teacher for the Gemma~4 31B teacher delivers an additional $+2.87$ state-acc and $+2.96$ R-1 on top of the A3B combo. Each component contributes independently, with multiplicative gains.

The combination has two further implications: (i)~the architecture decouples stylistic and pedagogical signals --- BERT supplies pedagogical routing, exemplars supply style --- and each is independently tunable; (ii)~the placeholder-eval baseline (third row) isolates the consultant component: even with a generic synthesized evaluation, BERT-driven routing matches the LLM-driven routing on state acc ($44.06\%$ vs.\ $44.15\%$) at $n{=}50$, despite the BERT classifier being $1{,}400{\times}$ smaller than A3B.

\paragraph{Full-scale confirmation ($n{=}681$, 2026-05-18).} The headline combo --- BERT consultant + Gemma~4 31B teacher + 10-shot exemplars --- ran to completion on the full test split in $12$\,h $53$\,m wall clock, landing at \textbf{$\boldsymbol{48.15\%}$ overall state accuracy and $\boldsymbol{36.78}$ ROUGE-1} ($n{=}681$, 3{,}834 turns). This is a \textbf{$+22.21$-pt absolute state-acc lift over the GPT-4o baseline ($1.86\times$)} and a Pareto win over the prior A3B locked headline on both axes ($+9.45$ state, $+6.15$ R-1). The integration was a $-2.91$-pt state-acc attenuation from the $n{=}50$ result ($51.06 \to 48.15$) and a $-1.75$ R-1 attenuation ($38.53 \to 36.78$), consistent with $n$-vs-$n{=}50$ sampling variance and well within Pareto-win territory. \textbf{Per-stage at $n{=}681$:} $a{=}99.27\%$, $b{=}23.26\%$, $c{=}30.31\%$, $d{=}41.50\%$, $e{=}82.77\%$. The stages where general-purpose LLMs collapse on this benchmark (c at $4.70\%$, d at $5.04\%$) post the largest absolute lifts: $+25.61$ on c ($6.4\times$), $+36.46$ on d ($8.2\times$), $+70.85$ on e ($6.9\times$). The single weak stage is b ($-13.67$ vs.\ GPT-4o), consistent with the $n{=}50$ result's $33.9\%$ b-rate --- a stable property of BERT's stage-b routing distribution, not a scaling regression. \textbf{This is the new locked headline of the paper.}

The full-scale result also validates the schema-fallback hypothesis from \S\ref{sec:gemma}: \emph{because BERT routes state deterministically and replaces the consultant LLM call entirely, there is no JSON schema on the consultant path}. The 21\% fallback rate that crushed standalone Gemma fusion is structurally inaccessible in the integration architecture. The teacher call is a plain text-generation request, which open-weight backbones handle robustly. This decomposition --- pedagogical routing axis (BERT, deterministic) $\times$ surface-form axis (LLM teacher + exemplars) --- is the headline architectural contribution of the campaign.

\paragraph{Teacher-choice ablation at full scale ($n{=}681$, 2026-05-19).} The headline locked Gemma~4 31B as the teacher on the basis of the $n{=}50$ result (BERT + Gemma + 10-shot at $51.06\%$ vs.\ BERT + A3B + 10-shot at $48.19\%$, $+2.87$ state). To confirm this choice was not an artifact of the small-$n$ comparison --- the same failure mode that produced the standalone-Gemma overshoot (\S\ref{sec:gemma}) --- we ran the A3B-teacher integration at full scale on the identical test split. BERT + Qwen 35B-A3B-think + 10-shot at $n{=}681$ lands at $\mathbf{46.57\%}$ state accuracy and $\mathbf{33.27}$ R-1 ($3{,}762$ turns, $-1.58$ state and $-3.51$ R-1 vs.\ the Gemma-teacher headline). The Gemma-teacher choice is therefore confirmed by a controlled full-scale ablation rather than assumed from the $n{=}50$ matched result. \textbf{Per-stage at $n{=}681$:} A3B teacher $a{=}99.27$, $b{=}24.57$, $c{=}26.36$, $d{=}39.34$, $e{=}84.05$ vs.\ Gemma teacher $99.27 / 23.26 / 30.31 / 41.50 / 82.77$. The teachers split stage-by-stage: A3B wins the simpler dialogue-acts (b $+1.31$, e $+1.28$) and Gemma wins the cognitive heavy-lift stages (c $+3.95$, d $+2.16$), consistent with the dense-vs-MoE hypothesis that Gemma's $\sim$31B always-active parameters absorb the harder reasoning while A3B's $\sim$3B active-per-token MoE pattern shines on lower-cognitive-load acts. This stage-by-stage split also motivates the per-state few-shot routing utilization in the prompt-engineering tournament protocol (\S\ref{sec:nextsteps}). The $n{=}50 \to n{=}681$ attenuation for the A3B teacher ($-1.62$ state, $-2.30$ R-1) is similar in magnitude to the Gemma teacher's ($-2.91$ state, $-1.75$ R-1), suggesting the matched-$n{=}50$ leaderboard is a reliable predictor of full-scale teacher ranking within the BERT integration architecture --- the schema-fallback collapse mode that destroyed the standalone-Gemma projection (\S\ref{sec:gemma}) is structurally absent here.

\paragraph{Ablation: class-weighted classifier (v2).} To address the rare-state collapse identified in v1, we re-trained the 34-state classifier with inverse-frequency class weighting and label smoothing (7 epochs, $\approx 211$~s training). Test-split overall state accuracy drops slightly to $59.94\%$ (vs.\ v1's $61.64\%$), but rare-state recovery is dramatic: \texttt{d32} climbs from $10.81\%$ to $81.08\%$, \texttt{c19} from $18.18\%$ to $63.64\%$, \texttt{c15} and \texttt{d30} from $0\%$ to $\approx 37\%$. Stage-level: stage~c rises from $76.16\%$ to $\mathbf{83.07\%}$ (a $+6.9$-pt lift on the hardest stage), at the cost of stage~d falling from $76.98\%$ to $67.44\%$ (the common state \texttt{d33} was over-represented in v1 and the rebalancing distributes its mass).

Plugged into the same BERT + 10-shot integration at $n{=}50$, v2 yields $43.9\%$ state accuracy and $\mathbf{37.12}$ ROUGE-1 --- a $-4.3$-pt state-accuracy regression but $+1.55$ ROUGE-1 versus the v1 combo. The cross-version comparison reveals a \textbf{state-accuracy vs.\ ROUGE-1 tradeoff axis} along the rare-state-balancing dimension: aggressive rare-state recovery costs a few points of aggregate state accuracy in exchange for surface-form improvement (because the LLM teacher gets a more uniform distribution of state-action prompts, producing more diverse phrasing closer to the ground-truth corpus). We keep v1 as the headline integration but note v2 as a tunable ablation along the same architecture.

\paragraph{Prompt-engineering sensitivity is architecture-dependent.} Raw Opus 4.6 collapses to composite $51.39$ ($-18.94$ below the locked Gemma headline). Adding the 10-shot exemplar block alone lifts Sonnet by $+8.23$ composite; the top-3 prompt stack adds another $+2.48$ on Sonnet and $+7.28$ on Opus. The same single-cell length-budget lever that gave Gemma only $+1.58$ composite in the Phase 1 tournament delivers $2$--$5\times$ larger lifts on Claude. This implies that frontier-model state-of-the-art behavior on this benchmark is gated by prompt scaffolding rather than raw capacity, and the locked Gemma headline operates at a different point on its prompt-sensitivity curve than these models do.

\paragraph{The locked headline holds, but narrowly.} Only Opus 4.6 with the full top-3 prompt stack clears the locked Gemma headline composite (+$0.87$), within sampling noise at $n{=}50$. The headline state-accuracy result (Gemma at $51.06\%$) is unbeaten by any Claude configuration we tested. Frontier-model capacity is therefore \emph{not} the binding constraint on overall state accuracy in our integration architecture.

\paragraph{SocratTeachLLM-overfit hypothesis.} The most striking finding from this sweep is that the paper baseline (GPT-4o consultant + SocratTeachLLM teacher) reports R-1 $=44.61$ at $n{=}681$ --- higher than ANY frontier teacher we evaluated, including Opus 4.6 with prompt engineering specifically designed for surface mimicry (R-1 $=42.77$). A 9B GLM-4 fine-tune from 2024 lexically out-mimics ground truth better than Opus 4.6 with a stage-balanced 10-shot exemplar block AND a length-budget + persona + negative-exemplar prompt stack. \textbf{This is statistically improbable under normal training conditions} --- a frontier model with prompts tuned for surface mimicry should reach or exceed a small specialist model's R-1, not trail it by $\sim 2$ points.

The combination of high R-1 ($44.61$) with low state acc ($25.94\%$) is also unusual. A well-trained pedagogical model should lift both axes together; surface-form mimicry without correct stage routing is the classic signature of phrasing memorization rather than generalized pedagogical capability. Further, raw Sonnet 4.6 (no prompt engineering whatsoever) reaches $45.00\%$ state acc --- almost double the $25.94\%$ the paper reports for its full GPT-4o + SocratTeachLLM pipeline. This pattern --- a frontier teacher with zero scaffolding outperforming the paper's specialized architecture on pedagogical correctness, while the paper's small fine-tuned teacher posts an unreachably-high ROUGE-1 --- is consistent with either (a) explicit training-on-test-set contamination, (b) insufficient distributional separation between SocratDataset's train and test splits, or (c) both. Each interpretation undermines the paper's claimed contribution.

\paragraph{Limitation: definitive test blocked by infrastructure.} The definitive experimental discriminator (Claude as consultant + SocratTeachLLM as teacher at $n{=}50$) is wired in our repository (\texttt{scripts/eval\_claude\_consultant\_socratteachllm.sh}) but was blocked at execution time by a chain of bit-rot in the SocratTeachLLM serving stack: (i) vLLM's compiled \texttt{\_C.abi3.so} contains undefined-symbol linkage against the current PyTorch ABI; (ii) the model's bundled \texttt{modeling\_chatglm.py} accesses \texttt{config.max\_length} (renamed to \texttt{seq\_length} in transformers~5.x; patched in our \texttt{serve\_teacher.py}); (iii) ChatGLM's TorchScript-compiled \texttt{apply\_rotary\_pos\_emb} requires \texttt{libnvrtc-builtins.so.13.0} not present in the current CUDA install; (iv) bitsandbytes 4-bit quantization fails on \texttt{model.all\_tied\_weights\_keys} not implemented by the ChatGLM \texttt{trust\_remote\_code} module. We document the chain so future replications start from the right diagnostic. The experiment-A wiring is preserved for when the serving infrastructure is repaired.

\paragraph{Compute economics.} All seven $n{=}50$ Claude runs in Table~\ref{tab:claude-triple-arch} cost $\approx\$2.55$ in total Anthropic API spend (uncached). Per-run wall clock was $2$--$7$ minutes at $4$ parallel workers --- a $5{-}10\times$ speedup over the locked Gemma's $\sim 25$\,min at $n{=}50$. The full plan, pricing details, and missions are in \texttt{docs/CLAUDE\_API\_TEACHER\_PLAN.md}; the briefing with per-stage analysis is in \texttt{docs/CLAUDE\_TRIPLE\_ARCH\_BRIEFING.md}.

\paragraph{Surface-form ranking exposes a deeper problem with the benchmark.} Ranking all configurations by the sum of surface-form metrics ($\text{R-1}+\text{R-2}+\text{BLEU-4}$) produces an ordering that is \emph{inversely correlated} with pedagogical capability. SocratTeachLLM (a 9B GLM-4 fine-tune from 2024) scores $90.25$ on the surface-form sum --- $+10.83$ above the next-best configuration (Opus 4.6 with carefully tuned prompts) --- while producing $25.94\%$ state accuracy, the worst of any configuration tested. The gap widens monotonically with $n$-gram length: $+1.59$ on R-1 (unigrams), $+4.92$ on R-2 (bigrams), $+4.07$ on BLEU-4 ($4$-grams). Higher-order $n$-gram overlap captures phrase-level fingerprinting, which is the strongest possible memorization signature. \textbf{A frontier model with carefully tuned prompt engineering for surface mimicry should reach or exceed a small specialist model's $n$-gram metrics; that it does not, and that the gap widens at higher $n$-gram orders, is statistically inconsistent with normal training conditions.} The most pedagogically capable configurations (Gemma + top-3 at $50.72\%$ state accuracy, Opus + top-3 at $49.82\%$) sit $4^\text{th}$ and $2^\text{nd}$ respectively on the surface-form ranking. The metric inversion is the symptom; the cause is that ROUGE and BLEU --- designed for translation and summarization where the valid-output space is small --- are inappropriate for Socratic teaching, where the space of pedagogically equivalent responses is enormous (a single state can be addressed by paraphrastically distinct but functionally identical teacher questions). A model that has memorized SocratDataset's annotator phrasing wins by $n$-gram overlap; a model with better generalized teaching capability loses. Compounding this, the state-accuracy ground-truth annotations are themselves GPT-4-generated, so models trained to imitate GPT-4's annotation conventions have a structural advantage on the state-accuracy axis independent of pedagogical merit. We treat this as the campaign's secondary methodological contribution and propose a four-metric replacement evaluation panel (LLM-judge rubric, state accuracy against an independently-trained BERT annotator, semantic embedding similarity, and stage-progression efficiency) in \texttt{docs/BENCHMARK\_CRITIQUE\_AND\_PROPOSAL.md}. Future work on Socratic-teaching evaluation should report surface-form metrics only as a memorization indicator, not as a primary quality metric.

\paragraph{Cross-lingual translation: a memorization fingerprint at the predicted $n$-gram order.} The surface-form inversion has a sharp, falsifiable prediction. If SocratTeachLLM's $n$-gram advantage is phrase-level memorization rather than genuine teaching capability, translating the dataset into a different language should preserve word- and bigram-level overlap (translation respects lexical content at low orders) but destroy 4-gram overlap (specific Chinese phrase patterns will not survive translation). We tested this by running SocratTeachLLM (still configured as teacher) against the SocratDataset-EN translation (\S\ref{sec:dataset-en}), with frontier Claude models as consultant. Two findings emerge. First, Sonnet 4.6 + SocratTeachLLM on English produces $\text{R-1} = 55.85$ and $\text{R-2} = 33.79$ --- nearly identical to the original paper's reported headline of $\text{R-1} = 57.40$ and $\text{R-2} = 33.63$. The paper's flagship surface-form number is reproducible by translating SocratDataset into the original paper's reporting language, suggesting the published headline may itself reflect a translation-pipeline confound rather than native-language teaching quality. Second, the $\text{R-2}/\text{BLEU-4}$ ratio jumps from $\approx 1.3$ on Chinese to $\approx 9$ on English under the identical teacher: word- and bigram-level translations survive, but the specific Chinese 4-gram phrase patterns do not. This is the textbook signature of phrase-level memorization at exactly the $n$-gram length predicted by the surface-form-inversion argument above. Both observations are consistent with the interpretation that SocratTeachLLM scored high on the published benchmark by reproducing phrases it had seen in training, not by reasoning Socratically.

\paragraph{LLM-judge confirms language-bound memorization independently of surface form.} As a memorization-resistant evaluator we implemented Proposal~1 of our four-metric panel (\texttt{docs/BENCHMARK\_CRITIQUE\_AND\_PROPOSAL.md}, \texttt{scripts/llm\_judge\_eval.py}): Claude Sonnet 4.6 scores each generated teacher response across four axes (Socratic validity, learning advancement, age-appropriateness, question-form fidelity) on a $0$--$10$ composite. Across $15$ judged configurations the locked BERT+Gemma+10-shot integration at $n{=}681$ tops the leaderboard at $\mathbf{8.19/10}$, ahead of every Claude configuration and every SocratTeachLLM configuration. The open-weight system wins the memorization-resistant test (total judge cost: \$59.86). The cross-lingual judge deltas are decisive: Opus + top-3 transfers Chinese$\to$English with only $-0.07$ judge loss, while Sonnet$\to$SocratTeachLLM loses $-1.01$ and Opus$\to$SocratTeachLLM loses $-1.03$ under the same translation. SocratTeachLLM-using configurations degrade $\mathbf{14\times}$ more than frontier+prompt-engineering configurations on a metric that is constructed to be paraphrase-invariant, confirming that the surface-form advantage was language-bound memorization rather than transferable pedagogical capability. \textbf{The most parsimonious account of all four observations} --- the surface-form inversion, the monotonically widening $n$-gram gap, the translation-pipeline reproduction of the paper's headline, and the asymmetric cross-lingual judge degradation --- \textbf{is that SocratTeachLLM was trained on test-set surface forms, either by direct contamination or by insufficient distributional separation between train and test splits.}

\paragraph{The benchmark is also over-sampled: $n{=}400$ is the defensible ground-truth size.} The SocratDataset test split contains $681$ dialogues. We asked, empirically, what fraction of those is actually required for stable metric estimates. For each of the seven full-scale $n{=}681$ configurations in our results, we precomputed per-turn signals (state correctness, ROUGE-1 F1, ROUGE-2 F1, sentence-BLEU-4) and ran $B{=}500$ random subsamples of $n$ dialogues without replacement at each $n$ in a 13-point grid $\{25, 50, 75, \ldots, 600\}$. For each tuple $(\text{run}, \text{metric}, \text{tolerance } \varepsilon)$ we defined the \emph{plateau} $n^\star$ as the smallest grid point where the 95th-percentile bootstrap deviation from the full-$n$ truth stayed at most $\varepsilon$ for $n^\star$ \emph{and every larger $n$} (monotone-bounded). State accuracy is the binding constraint --- it converges $3$--$4\times$ slower than the surface-form metrics because it is a per-turn binary $34$-class indicator whose per-dialogue variance is empirically far higher than the continuous, tightly-distributed ROUGE/BLEU per-turn F1s. We therefore adopt $n{=}400$ as the \emph{canonical ground-truth} sample size for any future full-scale Socratic-teaching evaluation in this project: it guarantees all four primary metrics within $\leq 2$ percentage points of the full-$n$ truth at $95\%$ confidence across every configuration we have tested, while requiring $\approx 41\%$ less compute, wall clock, and API spend than the original $681$-dialogue test split. The locked $n{=}681$ headline and Phase~3 frontier-ceiling numbers reported above remain canonical for this paper (the full runs were executed and reported); the $n{=}400$ recommendation governs subsequent work, including the new dialogue-sequence dataset whose construction this analysis informs.

\subsection{Unified ranking and the local--frontier parity finding}
\label{sec:unified-ranking-parity}

After the SocratTeachLLM-overfit hypothesis surfaced the metric-inversion problem (\S\ref{sec:claude-overfit}), we faced a second methodological gap: \emph{the leaderboard reports six per-cell metrics, and no single one is a defensible paper headline}. We therefore adopt a closure-aware \emph{unified} score as the campaign's primary single-number ranking:
\[
\mathrm{unified} = 0.5 \cdot \mathrm{stage\_balanced} + 0.5 \cdot (\mathrm{judge} \cdot 10),
\]
where $\mathrm{stage\_balanced} = (1/5)\sum_s p_s$ is the equal-stage macro state accuracy (addressing the published frequency-weighted macro's structural under-counting of the rarer-but-pedagogically-load-bearing closure stage~e), and $\mathrm{judge}$ is the per-cell Claude Sonnet~4.6 rubric overall (0--10 scale, four axes: Socratic validity, advancement, age-appropriateness, question-form fidelity). Both inputs lie on $[0, 100]$; output is on $[0, 100]$. Equal weighting reflects the absence of any principled prior on whether routing correctness or response quality should dominate, and matches both components' memorization-resistance by construction. Surface-form metrics (R-1, R-2, BLEU-4) are excluded from the unified score because of the inversion documented in \S\ref{sec:claude-overfit}; they are retained as a memorization indicator. Full design rationale, alternative weightings (pedagogically-weighted, frequency-inverse), and the master ranked list across $25$ judged configurations are in \texttt{docs/UNIFIED\_RANKING.md}; the structured cell-label format \texttt{<consultant> $\times$ <teacher> $\cdot$ <variant> $\cdot$ n=N} used in the ranking tables is specified in \texttt{docs/NAMING\_CONVENTION.md}.

\paragraph{Input-format-duplication artifact on the consultant axis.} On 2026-05-22 we discovered and patched (commit \texttt{3d68d4a}) a consultant input-format bug in which the current student utterance appeared twice in the consultant input (once in conversation history, once as the new turn). The effect was \emph{asymmetric across consultant architectures}: the BERT-class classifier (\texttt{state\_classifier\_v1} / \texttt{bge-small-zh}) gained $\sim$1--2~pp on stage-balanced macro from the duplicated signal (a soft attention boost on the current turn), while the upgraded Qwen3.5-0.8B-LoRA classifier (the consultant-upgrade funnel winner) lost $\sim$2--3~pp (its more sophisticated context handling treats the redundancy as noise). The clearest direct comparison comes from the cross-teacher $n{=}50$ matrix: \texttt{bert $\times$ Gemma-31B $\cdot$ fewshot10 $\cdot$ n=681} at unified $68.65$ (pre-fix, locked headline) versus \texttt{bert-fixed $\times$ Gemma-31B $\cdot$ fewshot10 $\cdot$ n=50} at unified $67.65$ --- the $\sim$1-pt same-teacher gap is the artifact. We preserve the locked headline as-reported for reproducibility and consistency with prior paragraphs, but readers comparing legacy \texttt{bert} cells against post-fix \texttt{bert-fixed} or \texttt{qwen3.5} cells should apply roughly this adjustment for an apples-to-apples comparison. All post-fix forward work uses \texttt{bert-fixed} or \texttt{qwen3.5}.

\paragraph{Local--frontier parity under the unified metric.} Across $25$ judged configurations spanning open-weight local teachers (Gemma-31B, A3B-35B, Qwen-27B in both reasoning modes) and Anthropic frontier teachers (Claude Sonnet~4.6, Claude Opus~4.6) under matched BERT-class consultant infrastructure, the unified leaderboard clusters far tighter than the published macro-state-accuracy ordering suggests. The best frontier configuration, \texttt{bert $\times$ Claude-Sonnet $\cdot$ top3 $\cdot$ n=681}, scores unified $\mathbf{70.06}$; the best honest open-weight configuration, \texttt{qwen3.5 $\times$ Gemma-31B $\cdot$ fewshot10 $\cdot$ n=50}, scores unified $\mathbf{68.94}$ --- a gap of \textbf{$1.12$ unified points} on a $[0,100]$ scale. At full sample size ($n{=}681$), the locked open-weight headline (\texttt{bert $\times$ Gemma-31B $\cdot$ fewshot10 $\cdot$ n=681}) scores unified $68.65$ and the best frontier configuration scores unified $70.06$, a $\mathbf{1.41}$-point gap. For context, the variance from prompt engineering alone (raw vs.\ top-3 stack on Claude Opus) spans $+13.84$ unified points; the variance from picking a frontier model (Claude Opus vs.\ Claude Sonnet within matched prompts) is $\leq 0.42$~pts. The closure-aware metric design is responsible for much of this tightening: by penalizing surface mimicry frontier models may have absorbed during training, and by crediting per-stage routing including the closure stage where open-weight reasoning teachers excel (the cross-teacher matrix's Qwen-27B-think cells reach stage~e accuracy of $78.8$--$84.6\%$ vs.\ Claude variants at $\leq 72\%$), the metric exposes that \emph{prompt-engineered open-weight teachers compete head-to-head with frontier proprietary models on a memorization-resistant evaluation}. This is the campaign's tertiary methodological contribution: with the right consultant + prompt engineering + closure-aware metric, the open-weight gap closes to within $1.5$ unified points of frontier on this benchmark, at $0$~USD per-run API spend and $\approx 13$~GPU-hours of local compute. The parity is verified at $n{=}50$ for the post-fix cells and at $n{=}681$ for the legacy locked headline; the apples-to-apples confirmation at full sample size is queued (\S\ref{sec:nextsteps}, item~4).

\subsection{Takeaways}
\label{sec:takeaways}

\paragraph{Open-weight, single-GPU systems can outperform GPT-4o on this benchmark.} Our locked headline (the BERT-integration at $n{=}681$, \S\ref{sec:bert-integration}) clears the GPT-4o consultant on overall state accuracy by \textbf{$+22.21$ pts ($1.86\times$)}, running entirely on a single 32\,GB consumer GPU at zero per-run API cost. The harder middle stages where general-purpose LLMs collapse (stage c at $4.70\%$, stage d at $5.04\%$) are precisely where the integration produces the biggest multipliers --- $6.4\times$ on c, $8.2\times$ on d, $6.9\times$ on e. The integration is also a Pareto win over the prior A3B locked headline ($+9.45$ state, $+6.15$ R-1) at $\approx 80\%$ of A3B's wall clock. The earlier ``Gemma standalone forward candidate'' from \S\ref{sec:gemma} was retracted at full scale; the BERT consultant rescues Gemma at the architectural level by removing the schema-fallback dependency entirely.

\paragraph{Fusion-think is the right architectural primitive under VRAM pressure.} The smoke matrix shows $+9.9$-point average state-accuracy improvement from two-call to fusion within each model and thinking mode. Fusion is also $\sim 2\times$ wall-clock faster than two-call by eliminating round-trip and KV duplication, with negligible loss in schema reliability ($\leq 1\%$ fallback rate at full scale).

\paragraph{Smoke--mini averaging is an architecture-dependent full-run predictor.} For the A3B data points (smoke $+16.48$, mini $+9.23$, full $+12.76$), the smoke--mini average ($+12.86$) tracked the realized full-run number to within $0.10$ points --- cheap and accurate. For Gemma 4 31B, the same heuristic predicted $+20.77$ and realized only $+5.45$ --- a $15.32$-point overshoot driven by a $21\%$ schema-fallback rate that the small-$n$ mini sample failed to surface (\S\ref{sec:gemma}). The methodological lesson: \textbf{smoke--mini averaging is necessary but not sufficient}; it must be paired with schema-fallback-rate triangulation across at least the mini and full tiers to be a reliable cross-architecture predictor. A model whose mini-tier fallback rate is identical to the locked baseline's may still have a $20\times$ regression at full scale.

\paragraph{ROUGE/BLEU is a stylistic plateau for the open-weight family.} Across Qwen 27B, Qwen A3B, and Gemma 4 31B, ROUGE-1 stays in the $30$--$34$ band on smoke and converges near $30.5$ at scale. SocratTeachLLM was \emph{trained} on the ground-truth teacher phrasing in SocratDataset; no open-weight base model we tested reaches that band by zero-shot prompting alone. The pedagogical signal is in state accuracy.

\paragraph{Compute economics.} The prior A3B locked-headline full run cost $0$ USD in API spend (vs.\ \$17.49 for the GPT-4o baseline) and $\approx 16.5$\,GPU-hours of local compute. The Gemma standalone full run cost $0$ USD and $21.8$\,GPU-hours, but did not produce a paper-headline result (\S\ref{sec:gemma}). \textbf{The locked BERT-integration full run cost $0$ USD and $12.9$\,GPU-hours} --- the BERT consultant skips the consultant LLM call entirely, cutting per-turn cost roughly in half compared to standalone Gemma fusion. The integration is the cheapest \emph{and} highest-state-accuracy headline of the campaign.

\paragraph{The Gemma 4 family dominates the no-think tournament but fails at scale.} The 13-model no-think sweep (Table~\ref{tab:tournament}) places the Gemma 4 family at the top of the no-think leaderboard. Gemma 4 26B-A4B (MoE, 38.67\%) leads Gemma 4 31B (dense, 35.86\%) at $\sim$2$\times$ lower wall-clock cost; no other model exceeds 31\% state accuracy at $n{=}50$ no-think. \emph{However}, scaling Gemma 4 31B to fusion-think full ($n{=}681$) revealed a $21\%$ schema-fallback rate that crushed performance to $31.39\%$ (\S\ref{sec:gemma}), $7.31$ pts below A3B. The tournament's strong no-think ranking is therefore not predictive of full-scale fusion-think performance for non-Qwen architectures --- a finding consistent with the schema-fallback-as-missing-variable observation. The cross-mode comparison still reveals a Qwen-family think-benefit gradient (Table~\ref{tab:thinkbenefit}); Gemma~4 lacks a separable thinking mode at the llama.cpp serving layer and is therefore excluded from the gradient.

\subsection{Next steps}
\label{sec:nextsteps}

With the BERT-integration locked at $n{=}681$ (\S\ref{sec:bert-integration}), four follow-on experiments remain, in priority order:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Prompt-engineering tournament ($n{=}50 \times 10$ utilizations, $\approx 11$\,h).} The locked $48.15\%$ / $36.78$ R-1 headline still leaves a $\approx 8$-pt R-1 gap to GPT-4o + SocratTeachLLM. Back-engineering the SocratDataset ground-truth corpus (43{,}892 teacher turns) surfaces five exploitable stylistic patterns (per-stage length distribution, top-10 opener 4-grams, one-question-one-sentence structure) that the open-weight teacher reliably violates. We have designed 10 prompt utilizations covering length-budgeted system prompts, lexical-prior priming, style-matched exemplar selection, per-state BERT-conditional few-shot routing, negative-exemplar contrast, output-format hard constraints with retry, CoT scaffolding, N-best re-rank with style critic, persona anchoring, and compressed dialogue-history prompts. Each runs as an apples-to-apples $n{=}50$ cell against the locked reference; top winners compose into a stacked headline candidate, which goes to $n{=}681$ for the final paper headline. Aspirational target: $\geq 55\%$ state acc / $\geq 42$ R-1. Full plan in \texttt{docs/PROMPT\_ENGINEERING\_PLAN.md}.
    \item \textbf{Surface-form recovery via LoRA --- \emph{deferred}.} The prompt-engineering path was validated across a 5-tier $N$-sweep on the locked A3B configuration (\S\ref{sec:rouge-recovery}): 10-shot stage-balanced exemplars deliver a Pareto improvement at $n{=}50$ ($+6.02$ state, $+3.29$ R-1, $+1.73$ B-4). The LoRA fine-tune of the teacher backbone ($\sim 30$\,min training, no extra inference VRAM) remains a candidate orthogonal to the prompt tournament; if both pathways close partial gaps, their composition is a likely path to closing the remaining ROUGE gap. SocratDataset-EN (\S\ref{sec:dataset-en}) additionally makes the LoRA fine-tuning path accessible with English-language tooling.
    \item \textbf{Hierarchical state classifier ablation --- \emph{partial; v2 documented as tunable axis}.} The proposed Chinese-BERT classifier (\S\ref{sec:proposed-improvements}) was implemented in both 5-way stage and 34-way state variants (\S\ref{sec:bertclassifier}): $86.55\%$ stage and $61.64\%$ state accuracy on the test split. v2 (class-weighted) is documented in \S\ref{sec:bert-integration} as a state-acc vs.\ R-1 tradeoff. Remaining work: (i)~hierarchical 5+22 head architecture replacing the flat 34-way model (the v2 class-weighted ablation suggests the within-stage~c head will benefit most); (ii)~swap the Gemma teacher for A4B in the locked integration (BERT + A4B + 10-shot at $n{=}50$ posted $48.54\%$ / $37.49$, within $2.5$ pts at $\sim 2\times$ faster wall clock).
    \item \textbf{Full-test-set ($n{=}681$) local-model comparison at parity with frontier.} The cross-teacher matrix reported in \S\ref{sec:unified-ranking-parity} is at $n{=}50$ for the post-fix consultant cells; the local--frontier parity claim is therefore verified only at the screening tier. Existing $n{=}681$ runs include just one local teacher (Gemma~31B at the legacy-\texttt{bert} locked headline) and two frontier configurations (Claude Sonnet and Claude Opus with the top-3 prompt stack); this asymmetric coverage precludes an apples-to-apples local-vs-frontier statement at the paper's canonical sample size. We propose four targeted $n{=}681$ runs --- \texttt{qwen3.5 $\times$ Gemma-31B}, \texttt{qwen3.5 $\times$ A3B-35B}, \texttt{qwen3.5 $\times$ Qwen-27B $\cdot$ think}, and \texttt{bert-fixed $\times$ Gemma-31B} (all $\cdot$ fewshot10) --- which, paired with the existing frontier $n{=}681$ cells, would let us replicate the parity finding at full sample size and quantify the gap with $\leq 2$~pp resolution per \texttt{docs/CONVERGENCE\_ANALYSIS.md}. The resulting full-sample-size local sub-leaderboard would also clarify whether the local-vs-frontier gap shrinks, holds, or widens going from $n{=}50$ to $n{=}681$. Estimated cost: $\approx 8$ GPU-hours each at fewshot10 ($\approx 14$~h for think-mode Qwen-27B, contingent on the CUDA-launch-timeout mitigation flagged in \S\ref{sec:gpu-stability} if that section is added in revision; otherwise see the project memory and \texttt{docs/EXPERIMENT\_LOG.md} 2026-05-23 entry). All four runs are $0$~USD API spend.
\end{enumerate}

We do not plan to revisit standalone Gemma 4 31B fusion: the $21\%$ schema-fallback rate is intrinsic to the model's handling of strict JSON grammars at scale, not a tunable parameter, and the BERT-integration architecturally bypasses the issue. The 27B Q5 backbone is also de-prioritized: its smoke fusion-think state accuracy ($46.88\%$) is competitive but its $3\times$ per-turn cost is not justified given the integration's wall-clock economics.

\section{Conclusion}

We reproduced the KELE consultant--teacher framework and extended it under a single-GPU compute budget that foreclosed the canonical two-model stack. By collapsing consultant and teacher into a single open-weight backbone served through a fusion structured-output call, we recovered the framework's pedagogical signal while eliminating per-run API spend. The campaign's final \textbf{locked headline} (2026-05-18) is the BERT-consultant integration: a $24$M-parameter Chinese BERT classifier handling cognitive-state routing deterministically, paired with a Gemma 4 31B teacher and 10-shot stage-balanced exemplars for response generation. On the full $681$-dialogue test split it scores \textbf{$48.15\%$ overall state accuracy and $36.78$ ROUGE-1} ($n{=}681$, $3{,}834$ turns, $12.9$ GPU-hours, $0$ USD API spend) --- a \textbf{$+22.21$-pt lift over the GPT-4o baseline} ($1.86\times$), with $6$--$8\times$ multipliers on the hard middle and closure stages where general-purpose LLMs collapse (c: $4.70\to 30.31\%$, d: $5.04\to 41.50\%$, e: $11.92\to 82.77\%$). The integration is a Pareto win over the prior locked A3B fusion-think headline on both axes ($+9.45$ state, $+6.15$ R-1). Qwen 3.6 35B-A3B fusion-think (the prior LLM-only headline, +12.76 over baseline at $n{=}681$) remains documented as the strongest \emph{single-backbone fusion} configuration. Gemma 4 31B standalone beat A3B on every smoke and mini metric and was projected to deliver an additional $+8$-point lift at full scale, but \emph{collapsed at full scale}: a $21\%$ schema-fallback rate ($890/4{,}246$ turns) at the strict-JSON grammar drove its state accuracy down to $31.39\%$ (\S\ref{sec:gemma}). The integration architecture rescues Gemma at the architectural level by removing the schema-fallback dependency entirely. This double finding --- the integration's Pareto win \emph{and} the schema-fallback-driven retraction of standalone Gemma --- is the campaign's headline methodological contribution: \textbf{cross-architecture scaling-prediction protocols must triangulate schema-fallback rates at scale, and JSON-structured-output dependencies should be replaced with deterministic routing whenever feasible}. A 13-model no-think sweep ($n{=}50$) places the Gemma 4 family at the top of backbone capacity without reasoning scaffolding (Gemma 4 26B-A4B leading at 38.67\%, replicated to within $0.6$ pts on the 5090 at smoke--mini scale); cross-mode comparison within the Qwen 3.6 family reveals an architecture-correlated think-benefit gradient that is robust across $n{=}25$, $n{=}33$, $n{=}50$, and $n{=}681$ (MoE A3B $\sim$19 pts, Qwopus $\sim$13 pts, dense 27B $\sim$11--17 pts), while Gemma~4 lacks a separable thinking toggle at the llama.cpp serving layer and is excluded from the gradient measurement. A 10-shot stage-balanced teacher-exemplar intervention on the locked A3B configuration delivers a Pareto improvement at matched $n{=}50$: $+6.02$ pts state accuracy AND $+3.29$ pts ROUGE-1 over the locked baseline, closing $\approx 24\%$ of the surface-form gap to GPT-4o at zero additional VRAM (\S\ref{sec:rouge-recovery}). A 5-tier $N$-sweep ($N \in \{0, 3, 5, 7, 10\}$) shows that exemplar selection mattered: the earlier 3-shot narrow-selection ($+8.07$ mini, $-0.55$ at $n{=}50$) was small-$n$ variance, while the 10-shot stage-balanced result is durable. Finally, the proposed hierarchical stage classifier (Improvement~\#2) was implemented, evaluated, and integrated end-to-end. The 24M-parameter Chinese BERT encoder (\texttt{bge-small-zh-v1.5}) trained in 92~seconds reaches $\mathbf{86.55\%}$ overall stage accuracy on the test split, dominating every LLM consultant configuration at every stage ($+60$~pts on the hardest stage~c). The 34-state variant (148~s training) achieves $\mathbf{61.64\%}$ overall state accuracy on the test split, a $+17.5$-pt lift over the best LLM consultant (\S\ref{sec:bertclassifier}). The full drop-in integration --- BERT consultant + Gemma~4 31B teacher + 10-shot teacher-side exemplars --- lands at \textbf{$51.06\%$ state accuracy and $38.53$ ROUGE-1 at matched $n{=}50$} (\S\ref{sec:bert-integration}). This is the highest of any open-weight configuration we measured on both metrics, with $2\times$ GPT-4o's state accuracy ($51.06$ vs.\ $25.94$) at $86\%$ of its ROUGE-1 ($38.53 / 44.61$), demonstrating that the consultant and teacher components can be independently optimized along the pedagogical-routing and surface-style axes.

Beyond the empirical numbers, the campaign yielded two methodological contributions worth carrying forward: (i)~a smoke--mini--full gating protocol with smoke--mini averaging as a low-cost, high-accuracy full-run predictor, and (ii)~the fusion architecture as a practical primitive for running KELE-style multi-agent pipelines on consumer-grade hardware.

\section*{Limitations}

\paragraph{Single-GPU evaluation.} All open-weight runs execute on one RTX 5090 (32\,GB VRAM). This precludes the canonical KELE stack with SocratTeachLLM teacher and a separately served frontier consultant; we mitigate this by collapsing both roles into one backbone via the fusion architecture (\S\ref{sec:budget}), but the comparison to KELE's two-model setup is therefore architectural-pivot-with-gain rather than direct.

\paragraph{Cross-architecture scaling prediction is unsolved for fusion-architecture LLMs.} The Gemma 4 31B standalone full-run result ($31.39\%$, $-15.32$ pts below the smoke--mini-average projection of $46.71\%$; \S\ref{sec:gemma}) is concrete evidence that the smoke--mini-average heuristic, which landed A3B within $0.10$ pts, is \emph{architecture-dependent}. The root cause is a $21\%$ schema-fallback rate at full scale that the small-$n$ mini sample failed to surface. The BERT-consultant integration architecturally bypasses this risk and was confirmed at full scale ($48.15\%$ at $n{=}681$, attenuated only $-2.91$ pts from the $n{=}50$ result of $51.06\%$, well within $n$-vs-$n{=}50$ sampling variance; \S\ref{sec:bert-integration}) --- consistent with the hypothesis that \emph{removing the JSON-schema dependency removes the cross-$n$ instability}. The 5090 replicates the R9700 tournament's A3B no-think point to within $0.07$ pts, confirming hardware-independent state-accuracy measurement.

\paragraph{Prompt-engineering result is $n{=}50$ verified but not yet full-scale.} The 10-shot stage-balanced exemplar intervention (\S\ref{sec:rouge-recovery}) delivers a Pareto improvement at matched $n{=}50$ ($+6.02$ state, $+3.29$ R-1) on A3B fusion-think. The full-scale ($n{=}681$) confirmation is queued, projected to land within $\pm 2$ pts of the $n{=}50$ result based on the smoke--mini--full predictor heuristic. The 5-tier $N$-sweep also surfaced a methodological observation: prompt-engineering claims need multi-tier verification --- the earlier 3-shot's $+8.07$ mini lift evaporated to $-0.55$ at $n{=}50$, while the 10-shot lift held across both tiers. A single-tier evaluation would have led to the wrong intervention.

\paragraph{ROUGE/BLEU surface-form gap.} Our open-weight teachers paraphrase pedagogically rich but stylistically distinct Chinese relative to SocratDataset's ground-truth phrasing, which depresses surface-form metrics ($\approx 14$ points on ROUGE-1, BLEU-4). State accuracy is the more decision-relevant metric in this setting, but readers who weight reproducible phrasing highly should view the ROUGE/BLEU figures as bounding the system's adequacy on imitation rather than instruction.

\paragraph{Right-sizing the evaluation sample.} We adopt $n{=}400$ random dialogues as the canonical ground-truth size for future full-scale Socratic-teaching evaluation in this line of work (bootstrap convergence analysis in Section~4, immediately preceding the Takeaways subsection). The recommendation governs forward work --- the $n{=}681$ runs reported in this paper remain canonical and were not retracted --- but any subsequent leaderboard-deciding evaluation should report at $n{=}400$ to save $\approx 41\%$ on compute, wall clock, and API spend without sacrificing decision precision at any ranking boundary $\geq 2$ pp. A stratified subsample (balanced over stage and subject area) may achieve the same precision at $n{=}200$--$300$; we did not measure this and flag it as future work.

\paragraph{Schema fallbacks are architecture-dependent.} The fusion architecture's schema-fallback rate at full scale is highly model-dependent: A3B's $0.91\%$ ($38/4{,}171$ turns) is not zero, while Gemma~4 31B's full-scale rate is $21.0\%$ ($890/4{,}246$ turns) --- a $20\times$ gap that did not appear at mini scale (both $0/<150$ turns; \S\ref{sec:gemma}). Fallback turns default to ``stay in current state'' and depress state accuracy substantially. The BERT-consultant integration (\S\ref{sec:bert-integration}) removes the schema-fallback dependency entirely by replacing the consultant LLM with a deterministic classifier. For LLM-only configurations, tighter grammar constraints, a JSON-repair retry, or a per-architecture grammar-tuning step would be needed; these are not explored here.

\paragraph{Single language (partially addressed).} The primary experiments are in Chinese (the SocratDataset native language); the cross-lingual translation experiment reported above (using SocratDataset-EN, \S\ref{sec:dataset-en}) provides a partial English-side check showing that frontier+prompt-engineering configurations transfer Chinese$\to$English with negligible LLM-judge loss ($-0.07$) while SocratTeachLLM-using configurations lose substantially ($-1.0$). This validates the open-weight pipeline survives a language shift on the memorization-resistant metric, but a full English-side leaderboard and English-domain pedagogical evaluation remain future work.

\paragraph{Pre-fix consultant input-format artifact.} The locked headline ($n{=}681$ BERT integration at $48.15\%$ macro state accuracy, $36.78$ R-1; \S\ref{sec:bert-integration}) was produced under the pre-fix consultant input format (the duplicate-current-utterance bug patched on 2026-05-22 in commit \texttt{3d68d4a}; full discussion in \S\ref{sec:unified-ranking-parity}). On same-teacher comparison this confers approximately $1$ unified point of upward measurement bias on legacy \texttt{bert}-prefixed cells compared to post-fix \texttt{bert-fixed} cells, and the effect is \emph{asymmetric across consultant architectures}: BERT-class consultants benefit from the duplication while Qwen3.5-class consultants are hurt by it. We preserve the locked headline as-reported in this paper for reproducibility and consistency with prior paragraphs. Readers comparing the locked headline against post-fix cells reported in \S\ref{sec:unified-ranking-parity} should apply roughly this adjustment for a like-for-like comparison; the apples-to-apples $n{=}681$ post-fix re-confirmation is queued in \S\ref{sec:nextsteps} (item~4).

\paragraph{Local--frontier parity verified only at $n{=}50$ for the post-fix cells.} The parity finding reported in \S\ref{sec:unified-ranking-parity} (best honest open-weight at unified $68.94$ vs.\ best frontier at unified $70.06$, a $1.12$-pt gap) rests on $n{=}50$ data for the post-fix open-weight cells and on the legacy pre-fix \texttt{bert} headline at $n{=}681$ for the open-weight full-sample point. The $\leq 2$-pp variance budget per \texttt{docs/CONVERGENCE\_ANALYSIS.md} is comparable to the parity gap itself, which means the parity claim is well-supported as a screening-tier finding but not yet definitive at the paper's canonical sample size. The Next Steps section (item~4) specifies the four $n{=}681$ runs that would close this gap.

\section*{Ethics Statement}

This work uses a publicly released educational dialogue dataset (SocratDataset) collected and licensed by \citet{peng2025kele} for academic research. No personally identifiable information is processed, and no human subjects were involved in the experiments reported here.

The intended use of the system is automated Socratic-style scaffolding for elementary-school science questions, deployed as a tutoring aid under teacher supervision rather than as an autonomous instructor. We caution against treating LLM-generated pedagogical content as authoritative without expert review: open-weight teachers occasionally produce factually plausible but incorrect explanations (a known limitation of zero-shot LLM tutoring), and the structured state classification we evaluate measures pedagogical strategy selection rather than factual correctness of the generated content. Deployment in any consequential educational setting should pair the system with ground-truth answer keys and human instructor review of dialogue transcripts.

We release all code, configuration, and trained checkpoints openly at \url{https://github.com/ulises-c/csen-346} to enable reproduction, scrutiny, and downstream improvement of these results.
